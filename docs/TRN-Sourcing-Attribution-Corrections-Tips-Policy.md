## 1. Sourcing & Evidence Standard

*(Reference copy for Seed 01: "What Is a Receipt?")*

A **receipt** is a piece of primary or verifiable secondary evidence that a claim in a TRN story is built on: court filings, SEC and other regulatory filings, government records, FOIA releases, sworn testimony, official transcripts, financial disclosures, leaked or public documents we can independently authenticate, and on-record statements.

Every factual claim in a TRN Story File is labeled with one of four tags:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Confirmed** | Established by primary documents, on-record statements, or multiple independent sources with no material contradiction. |
| **Alleged** | Asserted by a named party, filing, or credible source, but not independently verified by TRN. Always attributed to who is making the claim. |
| **Disputed** | Contested by another party with standing to know — both positions are shown. |
| **Unresolved** | The public record does not yet answer the question. We say so plainly rather than filling the gap with speculation. |

**Minimum standards before publication:**
- Every panel or claim that isn't common knowledge links to at least one identifiable source in the Story File's Source Library.
- Single-source claims are labeled Alleged, not Confirmed, regardless of how credible the source is.
- Anonymous sourcing is used only when the source has direct, demonstrable knowledge and on-record confirmation isn't available without material risk to them; the reason for anonymity is disclosed to the reader.
- Opinion, analysis, and commentary are visually and textually distinguished from reported fact.

**What TRN does not do:** present allegations as fact, run single-anonymous-source claims without corroboration, or launder a rumor into "reporting" by repeating it across formats.

## 2. Attribution Standard

- Every document, dataset, or filing used in a Story File is cited by name and linked (or, where a document isn't publicly hostable, described precisely enough that a reader can locate it independently — case number, filing date, agency, docket).
- Panels and graphics carry an in-frame citation marker ("Receipt #1," "Receipt #2"...) that maps to the full citation in that story's Source Library — published alongside the piece, not buried in a separate archive.
- When TRN's reporting builds on another outlet's or journalist's original work, that outlet and reporter are named and linked in the piece itself, not just in a footnote.
- Paraphrase is clearly marked as paraphrase; direct quotes from documents or people are rendered verbatim.
- Corporate, government, and individual subjects named in a story are given the opportunity to respond where practicable, and their response (or declination to respond) is documented in the Source Library.

## 3. Corrections Policy — Living Story Protocol

*(Reference copy for Seed 03: "Living Story Protocol")*

TRN investigations are **story files, not headlines** — they stay open, get updated as the record develops, and carry a visible history rather than being quietly edited.

**What triggers an update:**
- New primary documents materially change or add to the story.
- A named party responds, files a rebuttal, or a court/regulator issues a ruling.
- A previously Unresolved or Disputed item becomes Confirmed (or vice versa).
- An error is identified in a published claim.

**How updates and corrections are handled:**
- **Material corrections** (a fact, name, figure, or claim was wrong) get a dated, visible correction note attached to the original piece — never a silent edit. The note states what was wrong and what the correct information is.
- **Minor corrections** (typos, formatting) are fixed without a public note but are logged internally.
- **Story updates** (the investigation moved forward) are published as a dated addendum to the existing Story File, keeping the reporting chain intact and traceable from the original piece.

**The strict rule:** Living Story Protocol updates are reserved exclusively for genuine, event-triggered developments in a specific investigation. They are never used as a content-calendar filler slot, and no update is published without a new, citable reason to publish it.

## 4. Tip-Submission Policy

*(Reference copy for a "Send Us a Receipt" / tips page)*

**Contact:** tips@thereceiptsnetwork.org *(pending domain verification — do not publish live until the domain is confirmed active; see note below)*

**What to send:** public records, filings, internal documents, data, or on-record and off-record information relevant to TRN's coverage areas (politics, business, geopolitics, technology, money, power).

**What happens to a tip:**
- Every tip is reviewed; not every tip becomes a story, and submission is not a guarantee of publication or of contact back.
- Material provided is independently verified against public records and, where possible, corroborated with a second source before it's used.
- A source's identity is not published without their explicit, informed consent — regardless of whether their information is used.

**Source protection — read before sending anything sensitive:**
- Standard email is **not** a secure or anonymous channel. It can reveal your identity, IP address, and metadata to us and to anyone positioned to intercept it.
- For low-risk material (public documents, tips that don't expose you to professional or legal risk), email is fine.
- For anything that could expose you to retaliation, do not send it from a work device or work network, and reach out first — in the message itself, not as an attachment — to arrange a safer method before sharing sensitive material. TRN does not yet operate a dedicated secure-drop system; do not claim otherwise in any published copy until one exists.
- TRN does not provide legal advice. If you believe sharing information could expose you to legal risk, consult a lawyer first.

**Editorial independence:** No individual or organization is granted pre-publication review or veto power over a story in exchange for providing source material.

> **Flag for the team:** the `tips@thereceiptsnetwork.org` address and any "secure tips" language should not appear as *live and functional* in launch graphics until domain/mail verification is actually complete. Keep the "(Pending domain verification)" qualifier wherever the address appears until it's confirmed, to avoid publishing a dead contact channel at launch.
